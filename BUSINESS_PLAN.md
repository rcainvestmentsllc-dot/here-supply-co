# Iron Compass Business Plan

Updated August 28, 2026

## The honest premise

Iron Compass helps husbands and fathers reclaim their attention, carry pressure more responsibly, and return to the people and parts of life that work and phones can slowly displace.

The business is not selling information. Information is abundant, cheap, and increasingly generated on demand. Iron Compass sells a clear sequence of human practices, shaped by lived experience, made usable in ordinary life, and improved through real buyer feedback.

The website alone will not make this work. A strong product, a trusted public body of work, consistent distribution, and evidence from actual users must grow together.

This plan is designed to create that business without pretending success is guaranteed, manufacturing authority, or requiring Chris to become a daily social-media personality.

## The problem beneath the phone

The visible problem is digital distraction. The deeper problem is fragmentation.

A man can be responsible, productive, and physically present while gradually becoming separated from:

- his own attention;
- the transition between work and home;
- his wife as a person rather than a logistics partner;
- his children’s actual world;
- friendship, play, faith, curiosity, and physical life;
- the ability to notice pressure before he hands it to the room.

The phone is often the easiest doorway into that fragmentation, but it is not the whole story. Workload, isolation, stress, habit, avoidance, and the absence of clear transitions can all contribute.

Iron Compass should never reduce this to “put the phone down” or blame men for living inside systems designed to capture attention. It should still ask men to take responsibility for what they can change.

## Who the first product is for

The first customer is usually a married father between 35 and 55 who works hard and carries responsibility. He knows his family sometimes gets what remains after work, alerts, and mental spillover have taken the best of him.

He is not looking for:

- a guru;
- a diagnosis;
- a life philosophy to perform online;
- an empty community feed;
- another productivity system to maintain;
- a chatbot answer he could generate for free.

He wants a private, credible, well-made way to use one practice tonight and decide whether it helps.

## The distinct promise

**Get your attention back. Then bring it home.**

Iron Compass does not promise perfect discipline, a saved marriage, or a complete personal transformation. It promises a clear practice for the moment when attention, work, pressure, or drift is taking more than it should.

That restraint is part of the credibility.

## Brand decision for launch

Do not rename the entire business before the first buyer-feedback cycle. Keep Iron Compass as the name of the body of work, make Chris Avera the visible human voice behind it, and use plain descriptive names for the free guide and working session.

This prevents a long naming exercise from replacing market proof. It also solves the current trust problem without asking Chris to perform as a guru: the work is called Iron Compass, the person accountable for it is Chris, and each product says exactly what it does.

The complete visual, language, photography, naming, and course-experience rules are maintained in `BRAND_SYSTEM.md`. Any new public page, lesson, product, or email should follow that system before it is released.

## Why this is different from an AI answer

AI can explain habit change, write a weekly meeting agenda, or suggest ways to be present with children. Iron Compass must provide what a generic answer cannot:

1. **A coherent path.** The buyer does not need to determine which of a thousand suggestions belongs first. Return, Lead, and Keep create an intentional order.
2. **A practice, not a paragraph.** Every lesson moves from a recognizable room, to one principle, to one concrete practice, to an adaptation, to an action.
3. **A real person.** Chris names what he noticed, what he tried, where he still misses, and what the practice is for. He does not pretend to stand above the buyer.
4. **A shared human tool.** The weekly conversation, child-led window, friendship invitation, and repair script happen between people. Their value exists in use, not in the originality of the information.
5. **Judgment and boundaries.** Each practice explains when it should bend, when it is not enough, and when qualified help is the better next step.
6. **A body of work that improves.** Buyer interviews, expert pressure-testing, revisions, and transparent version notes turn the material into a practiced curriculum rather than a static content dump.
7. **Optional human implementation.** Workshops and working sessions can eventually help a man apply the material to his specific week without pretending to be therapy or all-purpose life coaching.

AI can support the making of Iron Compass. It should not be the reason a buyer trusts it. Trust comes from specificity, usefulness, restraint, responsiveness, and evidence.

## The product architecture

### Free: the 15-Minute Weekly Guide

A printable fifteen-minute weekly check-in for a husband and wife.

Purpose:

- create an immediate, useful first experience;
- show the Iron Compass approach in one sitting;
- give search readers and Substack readers a natural next step;
- invite a voluntary subscription without holding the guide hostage.

Delivery: immediate PDF download from the public site. No account and no email gate.

### $29: Focus Protocol

A private, browser-based 72-hour attention reset with four practical moves, a short tracker, and a reflection at the end.

Purpose:

- solve one narrow, recognizable problem;
- create the first low-risk sale from a stranger;
- give a result quickly enough to remember and discuss;
- introduce the buyer to the Iron Compass teaching style.

Keep the price at $29 through the first 25 stranger purchases. Improve clarity, delivery, and proof before changing the price.

### $99 founding edition: Iron Compass Core

A private, self-paced visual course with three movements, nine lessons, progress, and a printable workbook.

1. **Return:** The Sanctuary, Hunter vs. Farmer, The Airlock Protocol.
2. **Lead:** The Emotional Thermostat, The Date Night Algorithm, The Floor General.
3. **Keep:** The Third Place, The Friendship Script, Mission Debrief.

The customer experience lives on the Iron Compass website. Gamma is a source archive and visual reference during migration, not the buyer’s classroom. Skool is not part of delivery.

Pricing gates:

- First 10 stranger buyers: $99 founding edition.
- After 10 buyers and at least 3 candid interviews: test $149.
- Consider $249 only after at least 5 buyers can truthfully describe a concrete use or result, the module introductions are complete, delivery is fully tested, and the sales page has permissioned evidence.

### Standalone mini-courses: available later, not six new choices now

The native course is built so each Core movement can eventually stand alone:

- Return: attention, focused work, and the work-to-home transition;
- Lead: pressure, marriage, and fatherhood;
- Keep: identity, friendship, and life outside work.

Do not put all three mini-courses on the main navigation at launch. Too many choices will weaken the simple ladder.

After the first 25 paid users, expose the one movement buyers repeatedly ask for as a $49 standalone course. Credit that purchase toward Core if the payment system can do it automatically and clearly.

### Human offer: Personal Leadership Working Session

Do not lead with life coaching. Pilot a tightly scoped service only after several buyers ask for help applying the material.

- 60 minutes;
- one current problem;
- apply one or two relevant Iron Compass practices;
- one-page written implementation plan;
- $150 beta price for the first five sessions;
- clear educational scope, with no therapy, marriage counseling, diagnosis, or crisis care.

### Scalable human offer: Iron Compass Workshop

After the self-paced products have evidence, test a live 60- to 90-minute workshop for a church men’s group, fatherhood organization, leadership group, or small company.

Possible topics:

- Arrive Home: the work-to-home transition;
- Attention Under Pressure;
- The Phone-Free First Twenty Minutes;
- Personal Leadership for Real Life.

Beta pricing can begin at $250 for a local or virtual group, then rise only when the workshop has a repeatable outline, participant feedback, and a clear organizational buyer. A workshop creates human connection and borrowed trust without requiring a permanent community.

## The learning experience standard

Iron Compass lessons should not feel like a slideshow pasted onto a sales site. They should feel like guided practice sessions.

Every lesson uses the same intentional structure:

1. **A familiar scene:** the concrete moment the man recognizes.
2. **The principle:** one idea, written without inflated science or certainty.
3. **The practice:** three or four steps that can be used.
4. **Make it fit:** adaptations for work, family, mobility, money, safety, and imperfect weeks.
5. **Use it now:** one action before tomorrow.
6. **One question:** a reflection worth writing down.
7. **A note from Chris:** a short lived example in Chris’s natural voice.

The native course adds progress, previous and next navigation, and a printable workbook. Module introduction videos should sit at the beginning of Return, Lead, and Keep once the preserved source files are located. Video supports the human relationship, but it does not replace the written practice.

## Content roadmap without losing focus

The current Core should launch before additional tracks are built. Future content belongs in clear tracks rather than being mixed randomly into the existing nine lessons.

### Current path

- screen reflex and digital distraction;
- focused work and unfinished mental load;
- work-to-home transition;
- pressure and repair;
- marriage connection;
- fatherhood presence;
- friendship, identity, and life outside work.

### Future path: Faith and Interior Life

Possible themes include Sabbath, prayer, attention, service, humility, responsibility, and returning to what is sacred. This should be explicitly faith-based and optional, not hidden inside a general product.

### Future path: Work, Risk, and Personal Leadership

Possible themes include making hard decisions, using fear as information, building meaningful work, responsible risk, boundaries, and leading without letting work consume the rest of life.

Build a future track only when existing buyers repeatedly name the problem. One strong path is better than a warehouse of unfinished content.

## Delivery that is simple for the buyer

The launch delivery should work like this:

1. A buyer uses the paid checkout.
2. Successful checkout redirects to the correct private Iron Compass course page.
3. The receipt or delivery email contains the same access link.
4. The buyer opens the course in a browser, uses the lessons in order, marks local progress, and prints or saves the workbook.
5. A short follow-up asks whether the buyer used the practice, what was unclear, and what changed.

At launch, these are unlisted private links rather than individual user accounts. That is intentionally simple but not equivalent to account-level security. Add authenticated customer accounts only after paid demand proves that the added complexity is worth maintaining.

Platform roles:

- **Iron Compass website:** storefront, public resource library, private classroom, workbook, founder story, policies, and customer experience.
- **Stripe-powered checkout:** payment and receipt.
- **MailerLite:** optional checkout plumbing and buyer delivery email while it remains the simplest verified route. It is not the classroom or brand home.
- **Substack:** public writing, discovery, and reader relationship.
- **Apple Mail:** human support, replies, refund requests, and partner conversations.
- **Gamma:** source archive and design reference until every useful source has been reviewed and migrated.

## Credibility without fake credentials

Chris should not imply that he is a clinician, academic, or accredited institution. He can build real credibility in ways that are more durable than borrowed letters after his name.

1. Publish a plain qualifications and limitations statement.
2. Cite primary or authoritative sources where factual claims matter.
3. Show the complete curriculum before checkout.
4. Give away practices that are genuinely useful.
5. Ask licensed therapists, pastors, fatherhood practitioners, and leadership professionals to pressure-test relevant material.
6. Make changes from their criticism.
7. Publish a reviewer’s name or words only with explicit permission.
8. Collect detailed buyer feedback about use, not empty praise.
9. Keep a simple revision log so returning buyers can see the course is maintained.
10. Refer people to qualified help when a problem is outside the educational scope.

The strongest honest identity is: **a husband, father, and business owner who noticed a costly pattern in his own life, built practices to address it, and is serious enough to test and improve them in public.**

## The findability engine

Findability is not a launch task that ends. It is the weekly operating system.

### Searchable problem library

Publish one excellent public resource every two weeks. Each page should answer one real question fully, use language a man would actually type, include a specific example from Chris, cite reliable sources where facts matter, and end with one relevant next step.

The first cluster:

- how to stop checking your phone at home;
- how to be more present with your kids after work;
- how to leave work at work before walking in the door;
- a 15-minute weekly marriage meeting agenda;
- put the phone away before the car moves;
- why men need a third place outside work and home;
- a weekly brain dump for a busy husband or business owner;
- how to calm down before responding to your family;
- how to reconnect with your wife when life feels logistical.

The site must retain canonical URLs, article metadata, a public sitemap, crawlable text, descriptive headings, internal links, author information, and structured data. Private paid routes remain excluded from search.

### Substack as the human voice

Publish one substantial post every two weeks and one or two short Notes between posts. Begin with something Chris actually saw, did, missed, or tried. Preserve the odd, concrete details that an AI summary would usually remove.

Every complete post should link to one relevant Iron Compass resource. Every relevant resource should give a reader the option to follow Chris on Substack.

### Borrowed trust

Send two personal, relevant requests each week to therapists, pastors, fatherhood writers, men’s group leaders, business coaches, safety educators, or workplace-wellness professionals.

Do not ask for a generic endorsement. Ask one useful question:

> I built this short practice for men who carry work through the front door. Would you tell me what feels useful, unclear, or irresponsible before I share it more widely?

Their criticism improves the material. Their voluntary share creates qualified reach. Both outcomes matter.

### Guest appearances without becoming an influencer

Pitch a specific useful conversation to small podcasts, newsletters, church groups, fatherhood organizations, local business groups, and professional associations. The topic is the practice, not Chris’s personal brand.

Good pitches:

- the first ten seconds after a father walks through the door;
- why “use your phone less” is not a usable plan;
- what a weekly marriage meeting should and should not become;
- attention as a personal leadership responsibility;
- the downward glance while driving and the setup that prevents it.

### Paid traffic only after measurement

Do not buy broad awareness traffic first. Once the site measures resource visits, guide downloads, checkout clicks, purchases, and refunds, test one narrow search campaign at $5 to $10 per day. Pause terms that generate clicks without useful actions.

### Search and AI visibility

Well-structured, source-aware pages can be found by traditional search and can also be cited by answer engines. The goal is not to game either system. The goal is to publish the clearest, most specific, most trustworthy answer to a real question, with a visible human author and a useful next action.

## The first 100 strangers plan

Friends can pressure-test the material, but they do not prove the market. The first proof group should deliberately include strangers.

1. Recruit 10 founding testers through partner referrals, Substack, and small groups. Use a private no-cost pass and ask for candid use notes, not a promised testimonial.
2. Publish 6 complete search resources over 12 weeks.
3. Publish the matching 6 Substack posts.
4. Make 24 careful partner-review requests, two per week.
5. Book 3 small guest conversations or workshops.
6. Reach 100 qualified site visitors per month before judging conversion percentages.
7. Get 25 stranger Focus purchases before changing the offer or price.
8. Interview at least 5 buyers about what they used, what they ignored, and what they would have paid for instead.

## The weekly operating system

This should begin as one focused half-day per week.

### Measure, 30 minutes

- qualified visitors by page;
- guide downloads;
- Focus and Core checkout clicks;
- purchases and refunds;
- replies and partner referrals;
- the search phrases that brought people in.

### Publish, 90 minutes

- write or revise one part of the next search resource or Substack post;
- keep it grounded in a real observation and one usable practice;
- do not publish filler to satisfy a schedule.

### Borrow trust, 30 minutes

- send two relevant review or guest-conversation requests;
- record the reply and any permission to quote.

### Improve the product, 30 minutes

- review one lesson, buyer reply, broken link, or delivery step;
- make the smallest change that removes the most confusion.

## The scorecard

These are learning thresholds, not forecasts or industry laws.

| Metric | First useful signal | What it tells us |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Qualified site visitors | 100 per month | Enough relevant traffic to begin learning |
| Free-guide use | 10% of guide-page visitors | The first practice is relevant |
| Focus checkout clicks | 5% of Focus-page visitors | The offer is understandable |
| Focus purchases | 2% of Focus-page visitors | A stranger will pay for the narrow solution |
| Core purchases | 5% of Focus buyers or 1% of Core-page visitors | The complete path has demand |
| Refunds | Under 8% | The sales promise roughly matches the product |
| Useful buyer replies | 3 in the first 10 buyers | Enough detail to improve and eventually quote |
| Partner shares | 2 in the first 20 requests | The free work is credible enough to pass along |

The first commercial goal is not autopilot. It is ten strangers using the work and telling us what happened. Automation becomes useful after a real customer path exists.

## Intellectual-property rule

Iron Compass may learn from durable ideas such as prioritization, values, environmental friction, implementation intentions, habit design, repair, and reflective review. It should not copy another author’s branded names, sequence, exercises, wording, stories, illustrations, or distinctive presentation.

Build original practices from primary research, lived observation, and Chris’s own examples. Cite sources when a factual claim materially supports the teaching. This is a practical editorial rule, not legal advice.
