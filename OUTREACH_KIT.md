# Iron Compass Outreach Kit

## What we are asking for

Do not ask strangers to endorse Chris or promote a course they have not used. Ask them to look at one useful free resource and tell us whether it is responsible, practical, and worth sharing.

The first resource is the Sunday Board Meeting guide. It gives a husband and wife a short weekly agenda for discussing logistics, friction, money, family, and what matters before those things pile up.

The public link is:

`https://ironcompassinstitute.com/sunday-board`

## One-sentence description

Iron Compass makes practical tools for men who want to protect their attention, handle pressure more steadily, and be more present at home.

Chris Avera is a husband, father, and small-business owner. He is not presenting himself as a therapist, doctor, or guru. He built Iron Compass from practices he needed in his own life, then turned the useful parts into simple tools other men can try.

## The first twenty people to approach

Start close to the problem, not with celebrity accounts.

1. Five licensed marriage and family therapists who publish practical relationship resources.
2. Five church marriage or men's ministry leaders with an active local group.
3. Five fatherhood newsletters, podcasts, or resource sites that accept reader submissions or guest resources.
4. Five small-business peer groups or leadership coaches whose clients struggle to leave work at work.

Use the specific note that fits. Read the recipient's work first and write one honest opening sentence about why the resource may be relevant to them. Do not send a mass blast.

## What to record

For each person, record only:

- Name and organization
- Why the resource fits
- Date sent
- Replied, no reply, or follow up
- Exact criticism or permission to quote
- Whether they shared the public link

Never publish someone's name, title, logo, or words without clear permission. A private compliment is not a testimonial.

## Weekly rhythm

This can stay quiet and manageable:

- Monday: identify two well-matched people
- Tuesday: send two personal notes
- Friday: record replies and improve the resource
- Once a week: check visitors, guide signups, Focus clicks, and sales

Ten careful messages a month are more useful than a hundred generic messages. The goal is borrowed trust earned through a good resource, not borrowed status.
