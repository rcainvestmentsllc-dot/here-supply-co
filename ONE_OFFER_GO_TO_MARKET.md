# Here Supply Co.: one-offer launch plan

Status: current working direction, updated September 7, 2026. This document governs launch decisions. It does not authorize a live rebrand, domain purchase, checkout edit, email send, or production deployment.

## The four things to remember

1. **One parent brand:** Here Supply Co., pending a current domain and legal screen.
2. **One free entrance:** The Sunday Board Meeting, with the promise “See the same week.”
3. **One paid offer:** All the Way Here at the existing $99 founding price.
4. **One marketing path:** useful public stories lead to the free practice, the free practice leads to the course, and real use creates proof.

That is the launch. No catalog, quiz, paid middle product, subscription, group license, event business, or separate Focus Protocol.

## The problem

Work, screens, pressure, and constant input follow people into the parts of life they care about most. Many people are physically present but mentally elsewhere. They do not need a larger pile of advice. They need a small number of good practices that make it into real life.

Here Supply Co. helps an adult put work and screens back in their proper place, arrive more fully, and protect connection with the people and ordinary moments they do not want to miss.

The work is openly usable by women and men, whether single, partnered, parenting, or living alone. Chris’s clearest founder story is still as a husband, father, and business owner. The brand should not pretend he speaks for every life. Each practice includes a real-life variation instead of merely changing pronouns.

## The free entrance

### The Sunday Board Meeting

A free 15-minute conversation for two people sharing a life or household.

What the visitor gets:

- a public 3:12 video with Chris and Rhea;
- the real one-page, white, low-ink printable;
- four prompts covering connection, the household, the week ahead, and what they want to protect;
- immediate access with no account and no email gate.

The original full video remains private because it contains sensitive family material. The public cut ends before that disclosure and does not identify or use any child.

The practice does not need to serve every person. It is the clearest free door for couples and shared households. Single visitors can still enter directly through the public stories or course preview.

## The paid offer

### All the Way Here

Founding price: $99 one time.

Do not raise the price until real buyers have completed the course and described useful results in their own words.

What the buyer receives:

- The Attention Reset, a three-day opening experiment;
- nine visual lessons organized as Return, Lead, and Keep;
- one specific practice in each lesson;
- a “make it fit” variation for different schedules, relationships, and life situations;
- purposeful human-story artwork throughout the course;
- a 16-page printable Practice Workbook with an action page for every lesson, three family tools, and a 30-day integration page;
- a one-page All the Way Here Field Card that keeps Return, Lead, and Keep visible;
- an editable browser workbook with local-device autosave;
- private browser access without a course-platform maze;
- the existing 14-day refund window.

The course is educational. It is not therapy, clinical care, marriage counseling, diagnosis, or treatment. Do not promise that it fixes a marriage, treats attention problems, changes a child, or produces a medical or mental-health outcome.

## The audience

The public brand can welcome adults broadly. The first marketing should still use concrete moments instead of trying to name everyone:

- checking a phone while someone is trying to connect;
- arriving home while still mentally at work;
- carrying pressure into the next room;
- losing touch with friends;
- realizing that the household is together but not truly with one another;
- wanting faith, family, work, and attention to belong to one life rather than separate compartments.

Women need to see themselves in the art and examples. Men should not disappear from the founder story. Couples, parents, single adults, and friends can all recognize the underlying problem, but no sales page should claim that one course is equally tailored to every possible buyer.

## Why this can beat a chatbot

AI can produce more advice. Here Supply Co. and All the Way Here must provide what advice alone does not:

1. **A sequence:** a person knows what to do first and what comes next.
2. **A practice:** every lesson ends in one action that can survive an ordinary week.
3. **A human story:** Chris and Rhea’s real experience, believable artwork, and ordinary moments make the work felt.
4. **A place to return:** the course and workbook keep the ideas from disappearing into another chat transcript.
5. **Application:** any future support helps a person use the practice, not depend on a guru.

## Marketing without a content treadmill

Use four recurring story pillars:

1. **Divided attention:** the moment the screen quietly replaces.
2. **Work-to-home transition:** arriving physically is not the same as arriving emotionally.
3. **Shared life:** two good people can still be living different weeks.
4. **Real-life renewal:** friendship, faith, outside time, useful work, play, and the parts of life a screen cannot provide.

Each week, make one useful idea do several jobs:

- one source-grounded article or real founder story;
- one short Chris video based on that same idea;
- one email or Substack note;
- two or three strong visual excerpts;
- one clear link to The Sunday Board Meeting or the course.

Findability comes from answering specific questions people already ask. Priority pages include:

- how to stop bringing work stress home;
- how to stop checking a phone around family;
- a weekly couple check-in that does not feel corporate;
- how to pause before reacting under pressure;
- how adults rebuild friendship after drifting apart;
- how to create a work-to-home transition;
- how to stay present when life is busy rather than waiting for it to calm down.

Do not manufacture volume. One substantial human piece per week is enough if it is useful and findable.

## Proof before scale

Recruit ten founding users. Giving the course away is reasonable if the agreement is for honest feedback, not a positive review.

Ask each person:

1. What problem made you start?
2. Which lesson and practice did you actually use?
3. What did not fit?
4. What happened seven and fourteen days later?
5. May we quote your exact words with the context included?

Never invent testimonials, combine people into a fictional story, or imply a clinical result.

## Chris's coaching decision: apprenticeship before identity

Do not force a permanent choice between "hobby" and "high-level coach" before there is real evidence. Keep the public Here Supply Co. launch focused on the free Sunday Board Meeting and the one All the Way Here course, then run a separate 90-day apprenticeship test alongside it. This protects the work from becoming inflated marketing and protects Chris from building a new public identity he may not want.

The strongest first coaching niche is not "everyone who needs help" and not relationship counseling. It is:

> Business owners who are doing well at work and do not want success to cost them their attention, relationships, or the rest of their life.

Chris has lived business experience, access to business owners, familiarity with high-level real-estate clients, and a credible founder story around work crossing into home. That is a real starting advantage. It is not yet proof of coaching skill or client outcomes.

### The 90-day test

1. **Begin legitimate coach training.** Compare ICF-accredited Level 1 programs and choose one with live practice, observed feedback, ethics, mentor coaching, and a schedule that fits the two existing businesses. The current ICF ACC path requires at least 60 hours of coach-specific education, 100 client-coaching hours after training begins, 75 paid hours, at least eight clients, mentor coaching, a performance evaluation, and an exam. Do not buy a prestige program only for a badge.
2. **Hold ten research conversations.** Invite trusted business owners and high-level clients to talk about where business success creates strain in attention, home, identity, or relationships. These are research interviews, not coaching sessions. Listen for repeated problems and the exact language people use.
3. **After training begins, take five pilot clients.** Describe Chris accurately as a coach-in-training. Use a clear agreement, a defined goal, confidentiality, written scope limits, and feedback. Charge a real but founding rate so the test measures willingness to pay, not only friendliness.
4. **Use one narrow session format.** Working name: **A Private Working Session with Chris**. One current decision or pattern, one focused conversation, one written page of commitments, and one seven-day follow-up. Do not promise life transformation, business growth, marriage repair, clinical change, or unlimited access.
5. **Protect one half-day each week.** Do not destabilize the two existing businesses. Track preparation time, session time, follow-up time, emotional energy, referrals, repeat demand, and whether clients actually complete the agreed action.
6. **Decide from evidence at day 90.** Continue only if Chris likes doing the work, clients report a useful result, at least some strangers or weak ties will pay, and the work creates referrals without exaggerated claims.

### What high-level can become

High fees should follow a high-trust method, not precede it. The path is:

1. a narrow client and problem;
2. coach-specific training and supervised feedback;
3. a repeatable conversation and follow-through method;
4. documented outcomes in clients' own words;
5. referrals from people whose judgment the next client trusts;
6. a longer private engagement only when one session is not enough;
7. higher prices when demand, scope, and results justify them.

Chris does not need mass fame first. He needs to become known and trusted by a small number of the right people. A calm, discreet, non-guru coach who understands business and real family life can be more attractive to a private client than a loud online personality. Public writing and videos should demonstrate judgment and humanity. They should not manufacture celebrity.

The coaching industry is real, but it is not automatic wealth. ICF's 2023 study reported an average one-hour coaching fee of $244 and average annual coaching revenue of $52,800, while more than half of coaches reported under $30,000 in annual coaching revenue. ICF's 2025 summary says growth is expected mainly from more clients and sessions, not simply higher fees. This makes a premium practice possible, but only if Chris earns trust, specializes, and produces real client value.

### Scope and credential boundary

ICF credentials are professional coaching credentials, not mental-health licenses. North Carolina regulates the practice of clinical mental-health counseling and prohibits unlicensed people from presenting or practicing as licensed clinical mental-health counselors. Before any paid offer uses relationship, marriage, mental-health, or counseling language, obtain North Carolina legal review of the exact name, contract, website copy, intake, and referral process.

The first private offer should stay with decisions, attention, leadership, work-to-life integration, commitments, and implementation. Chris should not diagnose, treat, analyze trauma, manage crises, or present himself as a counselor. A responsible coaching practice also needs a written referral path when a client's needs move beyond the coach's training or agreed scope.

## What not to build now

- no separate $29 Focus Protocol;
- no subscription before there is a completed course and repeat demand;
- no email-coaching promise before scope, time, and boundaries are tested;
- no group host license before individuals use and finish the work;
- no church-specific edition at launch;
- no children’s screen course based on private family experience;
- no extra course simply because another good idea appears.

Later, the strongest low-complexity recurring offer may be a monthly application check-in or accountability note for past buyers. It should be tested manually with a few completers before becoming a subscription.

## Ninety-day launch sequence

### Days 1 to 30: finish and verify

- complete local desktop and phone QA;
- make all artwork physically believable and intentionally cropped;
- verify the free video, captions, PDF, workbook save, and print path;
- align checkout branding only after Chris approves the local preview;
- verify the name and domain immediately before any purchase;
- publish only after the local buyer path is approved.

### Days 31 to 60: founding use

- invite ten people to use the complete course;
- conduct short start, day-seven, and day-fourteen check-ins;
- fix the confusing or unused parts;
- collect exact buyer language with permission;
- publish two strong search pages and four founder stories.

### Days 61 to 90: sell the proven version

- update the sales page with real course evidence and permitted reviews;
- ask founding users to share the free practice;
- invite relevant podcast, newsletter, church, parent, and community contacts to review the free resource;
- test a small amount of paid traffic only after the page converts warm visitors;
- decide whether buyers want application support, not whether a subscription sounds profitable in theory.

## The only launch numbers that matter

- visitors who start the free video or download the guide;
- visitors who open the course preview;
- course purchases and refund rate;
- buyers who start within 48 hours;
- buyers who use at least one practice;
- buyers who finish the Attention Reset and the full course;
- exact phrases people use to describe the problem and the result;
- where each founding user or buyer came from.

The immediate work is to finish and verify this one path. The next business decision comes from real use, not another round of brainstorming.
