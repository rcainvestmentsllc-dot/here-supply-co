# Lesson 1.1: The Sanctuary
## Get the open loops out of your head
Iron Compass Core · Return
A simple weekly practice for a quieter mind and a more present home.

---

# This is for the man whose body is home but whose mind is still carrying the day
You may be thinking about the estimate you forgot, the text you need to answer, the bill you have not looked at, the conversation you are avoiding, or six small jobs with nowhere to live.
Nothing is wrong with you. You are carrying too much in a place that is bad at storage.
The Sanctuary is fifteen minutes with paper. It gives unfinished thoughts somewhere to land.

---

# What is an open loop?
An open loop is anything your mind keeps rehearsing because it has not been captured, decided, discussed, or released.

## Work
Tasks, promises, ideas, and problems that followed you home.

## Home
Repairs, schedules, family needs, and conversations that need attention.

## Money and admin
Bills, forms, appointments, purchases, and decisions.

## You
Health, rest, friendships, interests, and things you keep putting off.

---

# The goal is not an empty mind
The goal is to stop using your attention as a reminder system.

Paper does not solve the problems. It makes the problems visible enough to choose what happens next.
That is the relief: not having everything finished, but knowing that nothing important has to keep circling in the background.

---

# The Sanctuary
## A fifteen-minute weekly mind sweep

1. Sit somewhere quiet with one sheet of paper or one notebook.
2. Put the phone out of reach.
3. Write every unfinished thought you can name.
4. Do not organize while you write.
5. When the list slows down, wait one minute and ask, “What am I still trying not to forget?”

Stop when the page feels honest, not when it looks impressive.

---

# Use four simple headings
Draw four boxes or fold the page into four sections.

## Work
What needs action, a decision, or a boundary?

## Home
What have I promised, noticed, or avoided?

## Money and admin
What has a due date or consequence?

## Me
What would help me feel like a whole person, not only a worker?

Let one item appear in more than one box if that is how life actually feels.

---

# Give every important item one next move
You do not need a complete plan. Mark each item with one letter.

## D · Do
A small action you can schedule or finish.

## C · Choose
A decision only you can make.

## T · Talk
A conversation that should not stay in your head.

## R · Release
Something you are choosing not to carry this week.

Circle no more than three items for the coming week.

---

# What this practice is not
It is not a productivity contest.
It is not a place to write private complaints about your wife and call that progress.
It is not proof that you should handle everything alone.
It is not a substitute for professional help when anxiety, depression, conflict, or safety is bigger than a weekly practice.

The page should reduce hidden pressure, not give it a better hiding place.

---

# Bring the right things into the room
Before the Sunday Board Meeting, look at the page and choose what needs to become a shared conversation.

Bring:
- a schedule conflict
- a financial decision
- a promise that affects the family
- a place where you need help
- one thing you appreciate

Do not bring the entire brain dump. Bring the part that belongs to both of you.

---

# Make it weekly, not dramatic
Pick one recurring time. Sunday afternoon or evening works well for many families, but choose what your home can actually keep.

Keep the same notebook nearby.
Miss a week without turning it into a failure.
Start again with the next page.

A useful ritual is one you can return to without shame.

---

# Install it tonight
## Fifteen minutes
Get paper. Put the phone away. Write until the list feels complete enough.

## Five minutes
Mark each important item Do, Choose, Talk, or Release.

## One choice
Circle the single item that would make you more present tomorrow.

Success is not a perfect list.
Success is knowing what you are carrying and choosing where it belongs.
