# Iron Compass Course Audit

Working audit of the Focus Protocol, Iron Compass Core, companion workbook, and delivery sequence.

## Implementation status - August 28, 2026

The recommendations below were written against the original Gamma and Skool-era source material. The new native Iron Compass course now implements the central corrections without overwriting Chris's source archive.

Completed in the native course:

- Return, Lead, and Keep now organize the entire paid experience.
- All nine lessons have been rewritten into one consistent structure: a familiar scene, the problem, one principle, one practice, real-life adaptations, one immediate action, a practice kit, one reflection, and a short note from Chris.
- Unsupported precision, inflated neuroscience, alpha or warrior language, rigid compliance rules, and guaranteed outcome claims were removed from the native lesson text.
- Each lesson now includes plain words the buyer can use, a caution or boundary, and a primary or open-access evidence note where research materially supports the teaching.
- The work-to-home lesson separates driving safety from the parked-car practice and includes non-car adaptations.
- The pressure lesson includes returning when promised and repairing after a miss.
- The marriage lesson treats novelty as an experiment, includes the wife in the decision, and makes room for free, at-home, low-energy, and accessibility-conscious options.
- The fatherhood lesson makes physical play optional, protects consent and safety, and adapts the practice for older children and different bodies.
- The Third Place lesson credits Ray Oldenburg's original term, explains the broader Iron Compass use, and avoids presenting a hobby as a cure for burnout.
- The friendship lesson removes status language and uses a normal, specific invitation.
- The final lesson asks the buyer to keep two practices for thirty days instead of turning all nine into a compliance system.
- Focus is now a flexible 72-hour experiment with clear work, caregiving, accessibility, authentication, medical, and driving exceptions.
- The Core Workbook has been rebuilt as a concise native companion rather than a second course to manage.
- Three preserved module introductions now sit inside the private course home.
- Gamma remains a source archive and visual reference. It is no longer the buyer's classroom.

Still requiring real-world verification:

- exact captions or transcripts for the preserved videos;
- one controlled successful purchase for each product. MailerLite is configured with the correct $29 and $99 prices, private access URLs, buyer groups, and confirmation email, but a completed transaction has not yet been run;
- final MailerLite sender authentication for `ironcompassinstitute.com`. The domain has been added and the purchase email now uses the sender name `Chris Avera | Iron Compass`, but HostGator DNS authentication is still pending;
- replacement of the legacy Listings in Motion logo and generic body inside the MailerLite purchase-confirmation template. The buyer links and groups are configured, but this final email-branding pass still requires reliable access to MailerLite's embedded editor;
- feedback from invited testers and paying strangers;
- permission before any buyer words are used publicly;
- price increases beyond the $99 founding edition.

## Editorial standard

- Keep Chris's strongest position: a husband, father, and business owner sharing practices he needed himself.
- Remove fake precision, unsupported statistics, exaggerated neuroscience, and any claim that a practice guarantees an outcome.
- Replace commanding, shame-heavy language with clear responsibility, honest invitation, and room for an ordinary imperfect week.
- Keep one central practice per lesson. Move optional ideas into short side notes.
- Make the course sequence match the public system: Return, Lead, Keep.
- End each lesson with a small action, a reflection, and a bridge to the next lesson.
- Use real citations where research materially supports a claim. If a claim cannot be sourced cleanly, rewrite or remove it.
- State the educational scope plainly. Do not imply therapy, medical treatment, accreditation, or clinical authority.

## System-wide findings so far

### What is working

- The named practices are memorable: The Sanctuary, Hunter vs. Farmer, The Airlock Protocol, The Emotional Thermostat, and the 15-Minute Floor Rule.
- Most lessons lead to a concrete action a buyer can try the same day.
- The overall movement from attention, to steadiness, to relationships is coherent and commercially useful.
- The personal material is more persuasive than the high-performance language.

### What is weakening trust

- Several lessons still use the older `Attention -> Regulation -> Identity` and `Leadership Integration System` language while the current product uses `Return -> Lead -> Keep`.
- Multiple slides include statistics or neuroscience claims without a clear source. Some appear too precise or too broad to be defensible.
- Phrases such as `high performer`, `non-negotiable`, `no exceptions`, `the system that actually works`, `alpha male`, and `your command to execute` make the material sound scripted.
- Repeated tactical, military, wolf, warrior, and noir imagery pushes the brand toward a generic men's coaching aesthetic.
- Several lessons repeat the same action three or four times. The repetition makes the course feel longer without making it more useful.
- Some slides put too much responsibility for the emotional state of a wife or children onto the father. The course should teach influence, responsibility, repair, and boundaries without implying control.

## Lesson 1.1: The Sanctuary

### Keep

- The central idea that the mind is better used for processing than storage.
- Pen and paper, no organizing during the initial capture, and a weekly practice.
- The simple invitation to get open loops out of the head.

### Fix

- Remove the unsupported `47%`, `3x`, and `2.5 hours` statistics unless exact primary sources can be verified.
- Replace the claim that the prefrontal cortex `buckles` and avoid presenting general productivity advice as neuroscience.
- Qualify the handwriting claim. Handwriting can create useful friction, but the deck currently overstates what the cited research proves.
- Combine the three repetitive execution sections into one clear practice.
- Remove notebook and pen brand recommendations. Any usable notebook and pen are enough.
- Offer a ten-minute minimum for parents who cannot reliably find thirty uninterrupted minutes.
- Replace promises of better sleep and clearer focus with an invitation to notice what changes.
- Explain why the practice is called The Sanctuary and connect it to being more available at home.

## Lesson 1.2: Hunter vs. Farmer

### Keep

- The distinction between focused creation and necessary maintenance.
- Choosing one important target before opening reactive channels.
- Selecting the target the night before and removing obvious distractions.

### Fix

- Consider the clearer title `Hunt Before You Farm`. Both modes are necessary, and the lesson should not imply that maintenance work is lesser work.
- Remove the wolf, warrior, and `alpha male` imagery.
- Remove or correctly qualify the `23 minutes`, `3x productivity`, cortisol, peak morning cognition, and 90-minute ultradian claims.
- Stop multiplying interruption statistics as if ten emails automatically cost four hours.
- Replace the rigid 90-minute rule with realistic 25-, 50-, or 90-minute focus blocks based on work and family constraints.
- Remove `this order is not flexible`, `no exceptions`, and similar language.
- Tie the benefit to finishing the important work and arriving home with less unfinished mental spillover.
- Add a transition to The Airlock Protocol.

## Lesson 1.3: The Airlock Protocol

### Keep

- This is one of the strongest and most ownable concepts in Core.
- The parked-car transition, mental inventory, slow breathing, and deliberate decision about how to enter.
- Chris's personal field report. Lived experience is stronger here than performance language.
- The observation that distracted driving and work calls can extend the work state into the commute.

### Fix

- Reduce the submarine disaster imagery. `Everyone drowns`, `casualties`, and command language turn a useful metaphor into theater.
- Replace `non-negotiable`, `wins in both domains`, and `your command to execute` with measured language.
- Add a clear driving-safety step before the car moves: navigation set, phone out of hand, and no response while moving.
- Keep the actual Airlock exercise after the vehicle is parked and the engine is off.
- Add alternatives for remote workers, public transit, walking, and men without a private vehicle.
- Make cleaning the vehicle an optional environmental support, not a required part of the practice.
- Remove unverified exact personal details such as a heart rate of 110 unless they are true and intentionally personal.
- Add a concrete first-ten-seconds entrance practice: phone away, eye contact, and a real greeting.

## Lesson 2.1: The Emotional Thermostat

### Keep

- The thermostat metaphor and the practical before-and-after scenarios.
- The idea that a father's state influences a room without implying that he controls everyone in it.
- The pause before responding and the honest naming of a difficult day.

### Fix

- Remove the unsupported 90-second nervous-system mirroring rule.
- Remove or qualify claims that four seconds of breathing stabilizes cortisol, rewires a default response, or changes a child's future emotional template.
- Reduce guilt-heavy lines about children absorbing moods into sleep, the dog disappearing, or fathers teaching children how to be human.
- Add the most important missing practice: repair after a miss. Acknowledge it, apologize without excuse, and reconnect.
- Avoid promising unshakable calm. Teach a pause, a longer break with a stated return time, and repair.
- Remove duplicate threshold work already taught in The Airlock Protocol.
- Use one primary practice and make morning or evening reflection optional.

## Lesson 2.2: The Date Night Algorithm

### Keep

- The useful emphasis on shared activity, curiosity, novelty, and phones put away.
- The concrete list of date ideas and the instruction to choose a real time.

### Fix

- Stop attacking dinner and a movie. A familiar date can be connecting; the issue is attention and intentionality, not the activity itself.
- Remove `passive dates kill connection`, `you failed dinner and a movie`, `she notices when you phone it in`, and similar finger-pointing language.
- Correct the contradiction that labels an escape room as passive even though it is collaborative and active.
- Present `Novelty + Activity` as a useful experiment, not an algorithm that guarantees connection.
- Remove the unverified claim that the listed dates are field-tested unless Chris actually tested them.
- Replace `there will not be an emergency` with a realistic parent plan: allow priority contacts, identify the caregiver, and keep the phone out of sight unless needed.
- Add free, low-cost, at-home, mobility-friendly, and childcare-constrained choices.
- Invite the wife into choosing the date instead of designing an experience for her.
- Connect the practice to the Sunday Board Meeting so one shared plan can be chosen together.

## Lesson 2.3: The Floor General

### Keep

- The central five- or fifteen-minute phone-free, child-led practice.
- Getting at eye level, following the child's lead, and adapting the idea for teenagers.
- The existing consent and physical-accessibility notes.

### Fix

- Remove or verify the `60-110 phone checks`, roughhousing brain, oxytocin, daughter body-image, and 30-day transformation claims.
- Delete the invented week-by-week outcome forecast. It promises reactions that no parent can guarantee.
- Reduce guilt phrases such as `neither is an excuse`, `your kid will not be this age in 15 minutes`, and `your team is waiting`.
- Do not use `tickle ambushes` or `go hard`. Physical play must be opt-in, easy to stop, developmentally appropriate, and safe.
- Shorten the special section about daughters. Teach the same consent, cues, boundaries, and respect for every child without stereotypes.
- Make `the floor` a metaphor for joining their world. Older children, disabled parents, and children with different sensory needs may connect side-by-side instead.
- Clarify the apparent contradiction in `Floor General`: the father creates the conditions, but the child leads the play.

## Lesson 3.1: The Third Place

### Keep

- The recognition that a man can become reduced to work and household responsibility without a place that restores energy, identity, friendship, or play.
- Choosing a real activity, putting it on the calendar, and noticing whether it actually restores rather than merely distracts.
- Chris's own interests in mountain biking, fishing, kettlebells, and riding. These feel specific and human.

### Fix

- Acknowledge that sociologist Ray Oldenburg used `third place` for social community outside home and work. The lesson currently changes the idea into a mostly solitary refuge without saying so. Either explain the adaptation or rename the practice.
- Delete the empty research slide. A visibly blank statistics panel makes the lesson look unfinished.
- Remove the invented burnout equation and the promise that a third place prevents burnout.
- Replace `you do not need to work less` with a more careful observation. Some men do need workload, sleep, health, or professional support changes.
- Remove the arbitrary three-hour minimum, `no exceptions`, and doctor-appointment comparison. Start with a realistic recurring block, even thirty minutes.
- Replace `tell your partner, not permission` with shared coordination. Protecting restorative time should apply to both spouses, not become a unilateral announcement.
- Remove the contemptuous reference to `group therapy`. The course should not put down other forms of help.
- Broaden the examples beyond a macho garage, gym, or trail aesthetic. Include church, volunteering, a men's breakfast, a workshop, a fishing spot, a rec league, or a genuinely social third place.
- Bridge naturally to The Friendship Script: a restorative place can become a place of repeated connection.

## Lesson 3.2: The Friendship Script

### Keep

- The concrete, low-pressure invitation to do a specific activity at a specific time.
- Shared activity as an easier starting point than an abstract invitation to `hang out`.
- The emphasis on following through instead of waiting passively for friendship to appear.

### Fix

- Remove `high status`, `needy`, `social capital`, `hub status`, `position of strength`, and similar language. It makes friendship sound like influence strategy.
- Use a normal script in Chris's voice: `I am riding Bent Creek Saturday around nine. Want to join me? No worries if not.`
- Treat shared activity as a helpful option, not a universal claim about how men are wired.
- Remove `most dads tell me` and `most guys` unless Chris has actual customer evidence.
- Remove the arbitrary three-strike and three-to-five-interaction rules. Teach patience, reciprocity, and respect for a no.
- Make clear that one or two dependable friends can be enough. The goal is connection, not building a crew for appearance.
- Include fishing, riding, lifting, church, volunteering, breakfast, walking, and a small project as examples.
- Add the next step that is currently missing: after trust develops, ask one honest question and share one honest answer.

## Lesson 3.3: Mission Debrief

### Keep

- This is the strongest voice in Core. It is more reflective, less performative, and should become the tonal model for the rest of the course.
- Looking back at what changed, deciding what to keep, and choosing a small continuing practice.

### Fix

- Repair the broken `Click here` action.
- Recap all nine lessons, not only a partial set of practices.
- Remove `most men quit because it gets boring` unless there is real evidence.
- Ask the buyer to choose two practices for the next thirty days rather than making every practice a daily obligation.
- Remove the current undefined one-to-one offer about marriage and leadership at home, including the unsupported forty-eight-hour review promise.
- If a private offer is tested later, call it a `Private Working Session` or `Implementation Review` and limit it to applying the material. Do not imply marriage therapy, mental-health care, or broad life coaching.
- Invite honest completion feedback after real customers use the program. This is how Core earns proof without manufacturing guru-style authority.

## Focus Protocol Field Manual

### What is working

- The product is short, concrete, and points toward action rather than endless consumption.
- The four practices are easy to understand and can produce a useful first experience of intentional attention.
- A seventy-two-hour experiment is a good low-friction entry point into the larger Iron Compass system.

### Fix

- Frame the product as a seventy-two-hour experiment, not a detox, treatment, cure, or test of discipline.
- Replace `eliminate distraction`, `no negotiations`, `execute all four`, `do not customize`, and the strict restart rule with clear defaults and reasonable adaptation.
- Remove exact outcome timelines for sleep, conversation, focus, or family connection. Ask the user to notice what changes instead.
- Resolve the contradiction between a seventy-two-hour reset and a fourteen-day no-renegotiation period.
- Add necessary exceptions for work, caregiving, maps, authentication, medical apps, and true emergencies.
- Let the user choose a meaningful phone-free window instead of prescribing one household schedule for everyone.
- Add the clearest safety rule in the product: set navigation before moving and keep the phone out of hand while driving.
- Replace `attention belongs to my family` with `I choose where my attention goes`. Attention also belongs to safe driving, work, rest, and the person using the protocol.
- End with intentional reintroduction: decide which tools return, where they live, and when they are used. Avoid relapse and failure language.
- At twenty-nine dollars, include the short orientation video, the revised manual, a platform setup page, the five follow-up emails, and one simple tracking sheet. The manual alone currently feels closer to a nine- to nineteen-dollar resource.

## Sunday Board Meeting source material

### Keep

- It is simple, useful, and appropriately free.
- A repeatable weekly conversation is a natural entry point to the whole Iron Compass idea.

### Fix

- Replace `couples who lead together` with a more human subtitle such as `A fifteen-minute weekly check-in for you and your wife`.
- Start with one gratitude or bright spot before discussing logistics or problems.
- Add a fifteen-minute timer, one speaker at a time, one thing that needs care, and one thing that can be removed from the week.
- The one-to-ten connection or intimacy score needs guardrails: do not litigate the number, ask what would move it one point, let both people speak, and park a conflict that cannot be handled safely in the meeting.
- Make affection and intimacy prompts optional. They should invite connection, not create a forced weekly performance review.
- Keep the practice equal and shared. Do not imply that the husband is evaluating or leading his wife through an agenda.

## Core Workbook

### What is working

- It contains a worksheet for nearly every lesson and gives buyers somewhere to turn reflection into action.
- Several prompts could become useful one-page implementation tools.

### Fix

- The workbook still uses `A Practical Guide to Integrated Leadership`, `Own Your Attention`, `Lead the Room`, `Guard Your Identity`, and the older Attention-Regulation-Identity model. Bring it fully into the public `Return, Lead, Keep` system.
- The workbook is dramatically overbuilt. It contains repeated lesson pages, commitments, non-negotiables, daily trackers, weekly logs, thirty-day check-ins, and a ninety-day vision. This contradicts the promise that Iron Compass is not another system to manage.
- Reduce it to a cover, one `how to use this` page, one or two pages per lesson, one user-selected practice tracker, and one thirty-day integration page.
- Remove nervous-system contagion, `your family mirrors your nervous system`, and similar claims that overstate a father's control over other people.
- Remove scores and compliance language that turn ordinary family life into a leadership dashboard.
- Let each lesson page contain only: the central idea, the practice, one adaptation, one reflection, and the next small move.

## Delivery and product sequence

- The public sales pages should preview the practices and outcomes without exposing buyer-only welcome videos that assume a purchase has already happened.
- The Core overview beginning with `you just proved something` belongs after purchase, not on the public homepage.
- Public previews should use selected lesson stills, a short table of contents, and a new sixty- to ninety-second public introduction recorded specifically for someone who has not bought.
- Focus needs the same distinction: a public explanation of the seventy-two-hour experiment, then a buyer welcome that starts after checkout.
- The existing one-card `Focus Protocol Bridge` says `Protect What You Just Rebuilt`, `You broke the reflex`, and `Iron Compass installs leadership`. It is clearly post-purchase copy and should not be used as a cold public sales asset.
- Rewrite that bridge to match the current system: the reset gave the buyer a first experience of choosing attention; Core helps him return, lead a difficult moment, and keep a life outside work and feeds. Avoid claiming that a three-day product rebuilt him or installed leadership.
- `The Reveal` is a Twine & Co. jewelry asset, not part of Iron Compass, and should remain excluded from the course and website.
- Delivery should remain simple: Stripe checkout, immediate private access page, and email delivery. No community account is required.
